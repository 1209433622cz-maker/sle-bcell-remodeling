# Manuscript Pitch and Abstract Drafts

These are pre-results drafts. They define the intended story but must be revised after analysis.

## One-Sentence Pitch

This study integrates public lupus single-cell data with immune genetic and multi-omic reference resources to define pathogenic B-cell states in SLE and prioritize regulatory programs supported across transcriptomic, state-transition, and external regulatory evidence.

## Q1-Style Pitch

Pathogenic B-cell biology in systemic lupus erythematosus is often discussed through individual cell subsets or single candidate genes, but recent single-cell, genetic, and mechanistic studies suggest a more connected state landscape. We propose an integrative public-data framework that reconstructs SLE-associated B-cell states, tests their inflammatory and transitional relationships, and prioritizes candidate regulators by convergence across lupus state specificity, immune reference eQTL/xQTL support, gene regulatory context, and disease-focused mechanistic evidence. The expected contribution is not another cell atlas alone, but a ranked and interpretable model of pathogenic B-cell programs that can guide future experimental validation.

## Q2-Style Pitch

This manuscript presents a reproducible re-analysis of public SLE single-cell resources focused on pathogenic B-cell states. By extracting and re-clustering the B-cell compartment, scoring disease-relevant programs, and linking observed states to external immune regulatory evidence, the study aims to clarify how ABC/DN2-like, plasmablast-associated, activated memory-like, and APC-like B-cell states relate to one another and to known SLE mechanisms. The expected output is a practical candidate regulator and pathway prioritization resource for lupus B-cell biology.

## Draft Structured Abstract - 250 Words

### Objectives

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is characterized by heterogeneous immune dysregulation, and B cells are central to autoantibody production, antigen presentation, and inflammatory amplification. This study aims to define pathogenic B-cell states in SLE and prioritize candidate regulatory mechanisms using public single-cell and immune reference resources.

### Methods

We will assemble public SLE single-cell datasets and external immune genetic or multi-omic references. After dataset quality control and harmonization, the B-cell compartment will be extracted and re-clustered to resolve ABC/DN2-like, activated memory-like, plasmablast-associated, and antigen-presenting B-cell states. State markers and pathway programs will be assessed using differential expression, module scoring, and enrichment analysis. Candidate regulators will be prioritized by convergence across lupus state specificity, inferred state relationships, external immune eQTL/xQTL or regulatory support, and prior mechanistic evidence.

### Results

Results will define the major SLE-associated B-cell states, identify shared and distinct inflammatory programs, and generate a ranked list of candidate regulators and pathways for future validation.

### Conclusions

This work is designed to move beyond descriptive single-cell clustering toward a mechanism-prioritization framework for pathogenic B-cell states in SLE.

## Key Messages

- Pathogenic B-cell states in SLE should be interpreted as a state landscape rather than as isolated marker-defined subsets.
- Public single-cell datasets can be strengthened by integration with immune genetic and multi-omic reference resources.
- Candidate regulators should be ranked by evidence convergence, not by differential expression alone.

## Highlights

- Public SLE single-cell resources are organized into a B-cell-state-centered analysis framework.
- ABC/DN2-like, plasmablast-associated, activated memory-like, and APC-like B-cell programs are the main disease-state focus.
- OneK1K and CIMA are positioned as external immune regulatory references.
- The manuscript is designed to scale from a robust Q2 re-analysis to a stronger Q1 integrative resource if validation layers are sufficient.
