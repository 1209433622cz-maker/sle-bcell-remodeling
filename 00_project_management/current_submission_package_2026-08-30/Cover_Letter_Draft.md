Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, "Disease-blind single-cell reconstruction distinguishes unstable B-cell state assignments from reproducible interferon remodeling in systemic lupus erythematosus", for publication.

This study addresses a recurrent problem in disease single-cell genomics: cell identity, relative abundance and within-compartment transcription can be conflated when annotations are informed by outcomes or cells are treated as independent replicates. We reconstructed B-lineage identity without disease labels, retained biological samples or donors as the inferential units, and carried a prespecified transcriptional result into an independent SLE dataset.

The main contribution is an evidence hierarchy that separates unstable hard state assignments from a more reproducible process-level association. Fine naive-memory partitions were not stable enough for hard outcome inference. End-to-end reconstruction retained high global two-compartment concordance but missed the prespecified B_ASC overlap criterion; B_CONV and B_ASC are therefore presented as an analysis scaffold rather than a universally reproducible taxonomy. Propagating every observed broad-state boundary exchange preserved the primary antibody-secreting-cell composition null and both internal B_CONV IFN/ISG effects. The IFN/ISG program reproduced in the GSE174188 discovery contrast, a donor-nonoverlap internal contrast and source-label-defined independent GSE135779 childhood donors.

The manuscript also preserves two negative methodological boundaries. Repeated full-pipeline reconstruction did not satisfy the frozen two-compartment reproducibility contract and remains a permanent R1 HOLD. After normalization was corrected, source-label-independent external mapping did not meet its frozen reference-calibration criterion and remains a C9R HOLD; no corrected external disease outcome was estimated. Regulatory evidence from STAT1/STAT2 target activity, gene-set tests and IFN-response resources is reported as convergent but observational. We do not claim a discrete IFN-high subtype, universal plasmablast expansion, a causal regulator, direct transcription-factor binding, a unique upstream ligand or clinical utility.

All analysed datasets are public and cited by accession. The version-specific reproducibility archive is available at doi:10.5281/zenodo.22151739, with matching source code and integrity records at https://github.com/1209433622cz-maker/sle-bcell-remodeling. The submission materials comprise five vector main figures, ten supplementary figures, machine-readable figure source data, full statistical results and regulator-sensitivity records.
